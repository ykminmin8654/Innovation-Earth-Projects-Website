What we stand for

Four rules we don't break

Every product we publish has to pass these before it ships.

Free where it counts

Most of our tools are completely free — no paywalls, no trials. When something does cost money, the price is shown up front on its card. No surprises.

Private by default

No ads, no analytics trackers, no selling data. Most of our tools run entirely in your browser and never send anything to a server.

Zero friction

Open a link, start working. Accounts are optional everywhere we offer them — you should never have to sign up to get things done.

Built from real problems

Every tool starts as something one of us actually needed for a class, project, or competition — then we polish it enough to share.

Our story

From one homework tool to a studio

It started the way most student projects do: with a deadline. We needed a better way to get one specific thing done for school, couldn't find a tool that didn't nag us for money or personal data, and built a rough version ourselves.

Classmates started using it. Then classmates' friends. That taught us the obvious-but-easy-to-forget lesson: students don't want more accounts and dashboards — they want small, fast tools that do one job well and get out of the way.

Since then we've grown into a small studio that ships software publicly on this site — free or clearly priced, never ad-supported — and writes back to everyone who emails us. We're still students first, but we build for everyone: students, teachers, makers, and anyone who wants tools that get out of the way. That means we move quickly, admit mistakes, and build the tools we genuinely wish existed.

  1. The spark

    A single deadline-driven side project solves a problem our own classes hit every week.

  2. Word of mouth

    Students around school start using it daily — feedback piles up faster than we can file it.

  3. A real studio

    We formalize as Innovation Earth Projects, set the four rules, and keep shipping.

  4. Now

    New tools ship regularly on this site. The roadmap is driven entirely by what students ask for.
